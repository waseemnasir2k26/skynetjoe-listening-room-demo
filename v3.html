How it works

Three steps. No pressure at any of them.

Step one

Book a free 20-minute call

Phone or video, whenever suits you. You talk, we listen. We'll tell you honestly whether we're the right help — and if we're not, who is.

Step two

Make a simple plan together

One page. What you want to change, who needs to hear it, and what we'll do first. You stay in charge of every decision.

Step three

We walk alongside you

Sessions, appointments, letters, follow-ups. As much or as little as you need, for as long as it's useful.

Independent. Confidential. On your side.
About The Listening Room

Started by someone who knows what it's like to not be heard.

"Too many people in Leicester sit in appointments, wards and waiting rooms feeling like a case number."

The Listening Room began with a simple frustration: too many people leave with a leaflet and no idea what happens next.

I set this up to be the person I wished had been in the room. Independent — so I answer to you, not a service. Practical — so listening turns into letters written, meetings attended and questions answered. And patient — because being heard properly takes longer than ten minutes.

This is not therapy, and it isn't a crisis service. It's advocacy: your voice, made louder.

— Stephen Forde, Founder

Questions

Things people ask before they call.

Anything else? Just ask on the free call. There's no such thing as a silly question here.

Is this therapy or counselling?

No. The Listening Room is an advocacy and listening service, not a clinical or therapeutic one. We help you be heard, understand your options and take practical next steps. If therapy is what you need, we'll help you find it.

Is it confidential?

Yes. What you tell us stays between us, with the usual safeguarding exception: if we believe you or someone else is at serious risk we may need to involve emergency or safeguarding services, and we'll always tell you first where we can.

Do I need a referral?

No. Contact us directly, or a family member, carer or professional can get in touch on your behalf with your permission.

Is this the same as an IMHA?

No. If you're detained or liable to be detained under the Mental Health Act you have a legal right to a free Independent Mental Health Advocate (IMHA) commissioned by the local authority. The Listening Room is a private, non-statutory service and we'll always point you to statutory advocacy where you're entitled to it.

What happens on the free call?

Twenty minutes, no forms, no pressure. You tell us what's going on, we tell you honestly whether we can help and what that would look like.

Can I bring someone with me?

Always. A friend, partner, parent or carer is welcome at any session or appointment we attend together.

Where do you work?

Leicester and Leicestershire in person, and anywhere in the UK by phone or video.
